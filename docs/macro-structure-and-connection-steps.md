# Macro Structure and Connection Steps

One structure, used three ways: to speak in front of an audience, to connect one on one, and to show up online. These are the steps Marc Gray and Kristen McIntyre outlined for OCP 101. The site's three landing pages (`src/pages/audience`, `src/pages/one-on-one`, `src/pages/online`) carry the same content.

## 1. In front of an audience: the speech macro structure

Build the body first, then wrap the introduction and conclusion around it.

### Introduction: five parts

1. **Icebreaker**: grabs attention in a way tied to the topic. Never open with the title of your speech.
2. **Listener relevance link**: tells this audience how the topic affects them.
3. **Speaker credibility**: a specific reason to trust you.
4. **Thesis statement**: the central idea in one sentence.
5. **Preview**: names each main point in order.

### Body: two or three main points

Under each main point:

1. State the point in one sentence.
2. Support it with cited research, said out loud by author, outlet, and year.
3. Give an example that resonates with this audience.
4. Add a listener relevance link that ties the point to their own lives.

### Transitions

One at every handoff: from the introduction into the first point, between each main point, and into the conclusion.

### Conclusion: three parts

1. **Restated thesis**, in fresh words.
2. **Summary of main points.**
3. **Clincher**: a memorable last line, often tied back to the icebreaker. Never end on "So yeah, that's it" or "Any questions?"

### Persuasive speeches: Monroe's Motivated Sequence

For a policy or action speech, the main points follow: Attention, Need, Satisfaction, Visualization, Action. For an attitude or value claim, use whatever pattern fits.

## 2. One on one

### Open a conversation

Greet, ask a question about them, listen, paraphrase what you heard. All four before you say anything about yourself.

### The 60-second elevator pitch

Your LinkedIn hook, said out loud: who you are, your focus, your proof, then hand it back with a question. Adjust the emphasis for the listener:

- **A potential boss**: lead with your proof; ask what the team needs in an intern.
- **A useful connection**: lead with your focus; ask about their path and who else to meet.
- **A classmate or peer**: lead with your curiosity; ask what they are working on.

### Interview answers: modified macro structure

1. Thesis: answer in one sentence.
2. Preview: "Two examples come to mind."
3. Example one, and what it shows.
4. Example two, and what it shows.
5. Restate the thesis.
6. Stop talking.

## 3. Online

1. Fill out the LinkedIn worksheet in the workspace.
2. Rebuild the profile around one area of expertise.
3. Film every speech for reuse: tripod at eye level, centered in the frame so it crops to vertical, room above your head, a pause between main points.
4. Publish a short video portfolio and pin it to Featured.
